CHAPTER 1

The Keeper

The keeper climbed the ninety stairs every evening, and every evening the sea had something new to say.

On the night the letter came, it said nothing at all.

2

The envelope was dry. That was the first wrong thing. Nothing on the island stayed dry for long, not the bread, not the beds, not the stories the fishermen told about the ones who did not come back.

He set it on the table beside the lamp and did not open it. He wound the clock. He cleaned the glass. He watched the beam go out over the water and come back empty, the way it always did.

⁂

By the third letter he had stopped pretending not to know the handwriting.

CHAPTER 2

The Tin

He kept them in a biscuit tin with a painted ship on the lid, the paint worn through where his thumb found it each night.

4

There were eleven by midwinter. He knew the order without reading the dates, the way you know the stairs in the dark: by the weight of each one, by where they turned.

On the twelfth night the lamp failed, and in the dark he finally read one aloud.
